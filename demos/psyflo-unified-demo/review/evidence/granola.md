## 3. Topics the team must resolve

### 3a. Enhancing or editing old, already-processed notes versus Medicaid audit risk

For enhancing old notes:
- G-035: a PsyFlo idea (1 Nov) to let staff reopen a six-month-old note and generate an enhanced version.
- G-084: the same capability was described to an outside clinic on 6 Nov.
- No Harvest request for retroactive editing exists in Granola. The staff wish is help writing notes (G-009, G-025), which is a current-note problem.

For not touching saved notes:
- G-093: the 7 Nov decision to remove Scan and Enhance and to edit only in real time, because retroactive edits could create audit and claw-back exposure through timestamps.
- G-094: a speaker recalled a 24-hour entry rule and worried that late edits would look bad in an audit.
- G-011: Harvest is audited routinely (about every 3 to 4 months, about 20 client files), so audit sensitivity is real.

Read-only use of history is already part of the plan:
- G-090 and G-092: the continued-stay SRA reads old notes (61 notes, collaterals, guardian calls) and PsyFlo expects poor legacy DPNs to make early SRAs choppy. G-007 and G-008: licensed clinicians already read about 60 DPNs by hand.
- G-033 and G-036: edits to a current draft happen in the extension and the final text is the last entry in the EHR.

Not known: whether any DMAS or MCO rule bars a late-entry addendum; whether Harvest has an addendum policy; whether the audit-risk reasoning is correct (the speaker was unsure and nobody validated it). The Granola evidence is consistent with splitting current-draft review from read-only historical review, but the policy basis rests on one internal hunch, not on a rule.
